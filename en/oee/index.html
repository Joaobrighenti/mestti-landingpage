OEE: what it is, how to calculate it and how to track it live

By João Brighenti, founder of Mestti · Reviewed on 10/29/2026

Short answer

OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) shows how much of a machine’s planned time turned into good parts. It multiplies three factors: availability (how long it ran), performance (at what pace) and quality (how many parts came out good).

OEE = D × P × QE.g.: 90% × 86% × 96% ≈ 75%

The three OEE factors

  1. Availability

    Run time ÷ planned time

    Drops with stops, long setups, material shortages and micro-stops nobody writes down.

  2. Performance

    (Parts produced × ideal cycle) ÷ run time

    Drops when the machine runs below its standard pace or has short stops.

  3. Quality

    Good parts ÷ parts produced

    Drops with scrap, rework and parts discarded at the start of a batch.

Mistakes that distort OEE

  • Writing stops down only at the end of the shift: the time and the reason disappear.

  • Using the sales target instead of the machine’s ideal cycle.

  • Ignoring micro-stops: performance drops with no explanation.

  • Counting parts on paper: delays and typing errors.

  • Looking only at the factory average: it hides the machine that loses the most.

  • Comparing machines whose planned time is calculated in different ways.

OEE at the end of the day or live?

In the end-of-day report

  • Answers “what happened”.
  • The 09:42 stop shows up at closing time.
  • The reason is written down later, if someone remembers.
  • The loss has already become a delay.

Live, per machine

  • Answers “what is happening”.
  • The stop shows up right away, with its start time.
  • Whoever is at the machine gives the reason on the tablet.
  • There is still time to act during the shift.

Questions about OEE

What is a good OEE?

There is no single number. 85% is often cited as a world-class reference, but OEE varies with the type of process. The most useful thing is to compare the same machine over time and find the factor where it loses the most.

What is the difference between OEE and productivity?

Productivity usually looks at how much came out. OEE compares that with what the machine could have made in the planned time and splits the loss into stopped time, pace and quality.

Do micro-stops count in OEE?

Yes. Short stops reduce run time or pace, depending on how the factory classifies them. Without automatic recording, they tend to disappear from manual reports.

Do I need a sensor to measure OEE?

You can calculate it from manual reports, but stop times and counts become less reliable. With a sensor, the count and stopped time are recorded automatically and the operator only gives the reason.

Can OEE be measured on old machines?

Yes, in most cases. The Mestti sensor reads count pulses or the machine’s electrical signal, with no PLC and no changes to the controls.

Is OEE for the whole factory or for each machine?

Both, but OEE per machine is what shows where to act. The factory average hides the machine that loses the most.
