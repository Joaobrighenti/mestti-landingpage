AI in manufacturing: what already works on the shop floor

LE

By Leonardo Ernica, co-founder of Mestti · Reviewed on 2026-10-29

Short answer

In a factory, AI is useful when it works on reliable operational data: answering in plain language where production lost time, which orders may be late or what is pending in quality, and preparing actions for a person to approve. Without well-recorded production data, it only repeats what people already assumed.

Where AI already helps on the shop floor

  1. Questions

    “Where did I lose the most time this week?” answered with the factory’s data.

  2. Investigation

    Cross-checking stops, times and machines to find the pattern that repeats.

  3. Simulation

    Testing a sequence swap and seeing the effect on due dates.

  4. Actions to approve

    Preparing a task or a notice and waiting for a person to approve it.

The factory’s memory

In many factories, what is known about production is scattered: in the supervisor’s head, the planner’s spreadsheet, the quality notebook. When someone goes on vacation, part of that memory goes too.

A “company brain” means bringing all of this into one place: orders, engineering routings, stops with reasons, inspections and history. AI becomes useful when it queries that memory, not when it makes up an answer.

Precautions for using AI in production

  • AI should only see what the user is allowed to see.

  • Nothing should run without a person’s approval.

  • The answer must show where the numbers came from.

  • AI does not replace the system’s official calculations, such as OEE.

Questions about AI in manufacturing

Does Otto make decisions on its own?

No. It queries, investigates and proposes. Any action only runs after a person approves.

Which data does the AI see?

Only what your user is allowed to see. Each company’s data is isolated.

Do I need an AI project to get started?

No. The first step is measuring production. With the data recorded, Otto starts answering about it.

Does AI replace the planner or the supervisor?

No. It shortens the path to information and prepares proposals. The decision stays with the team.
