What it is

The fourth industrial revolution.

The term was born in Germany as Industrie 4.0 and was presented at the Hannover fair in 2011. It describes a factory where machines, people and systems stay connected and share data all the time.

In practice, Industry 4.0 changes what decisions are based on. The machine reports when it stops, the system calculates what was lost and management acts the same day, not at the end of the month.

  1. 18th century1st revolution

    Steam power and mechanization.

  2. Late 19th century2nd revolution

    Electricity and mass production on assembly lines.

  3. 1970s3rd revolution

    Electronics, PLCs and machine automation.

  4. From 20114th revolution

    Machines, people and systems connected, sharing data in real time.

Myths that hold back Industry 4.0

  • “I need to replace my machines.” Most old machines can be measured with an external sensor.

  • “It’s only for large factories.” Small factories feel every lost hour even more.

  • “It starts with a huge project.” You can start with one department and grow from the results.

  • “AI solves it on its own.” Without reliable production data, AI only repeats what people already assumed.

Questions about Industry 4.0

Are Industry 4.0 and MES the same thing?

No. Industry 4.0 is the idea of deciding with connected data. An MES is one of the systems that makes this possible on the shop floor.

Do I need an IT team?

Not to get started. The system runs in the cloud, opens in the browser and the app runs on the tablet. The network needs 2.4 GHz Wi‑Fi near the machines.

Can I start without integrating the ERP?

Yes. The ERP integration can come later, once production numbers are reliable.

How much does it cost to start?

The trial week is free, with the sensor on loan. After that, the price depends on the number of machines and what the factory will use.

Smart factory, MES and MOM: what’s the difference?

A smart factory is a plant that already decides with connected data. An MES tracks production. MOM brings production, quality, maintenance and inventory onto the same base. All three are paths of Industry 4.0.

Where does artificial intelligence come in?

Once production data is reliable. In Mestti, Otto reads that data, explains deviations and proposes actions, which only happen with a person’s approval.
